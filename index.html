Fair is the sharp books' de-vigged consensus — Pinnacle, DraftKings, FanDuel, Caesars, BetMGM, BetRivers — shifted to this platform's own line. Effective is Fair × Underdog's per-pick payout multiplier (PrizePicks pays flat), which is the number that compares across platforms. Model is our own projection, shown for comparison and never used to pick the row; it turns red when it disagrees with the market on direction.

Highlighted rows are verified by two or more independent sharp books on a platform with a fixed payout schedule. Rows priced only by the platform itself are listed but not flagged — that is the platform agreeing with itself. DraftKings Pick6 is peer-to-peer (no fixed payout) and is listed for its line, not flagged.

Line shopping — the same prop, priced differently across platforms

Each cell is that platform's line and the sharp books' probability at that line, after its payout multiplier. A softer line shows up as a higher number on the platform that posted it, so Gap is what you give up by taking the prop on the wrong platform. Pushes are handled: a whole-number line is scored on the legs that count, since a push voids the leg rather than losing it. DraftKings Pick6 appears whenever its capture is fresh.

Half-point vs whole-number lines are where most of this lives: an Over is easier at 3.5 than 4.0, and an Under is safer at 4.0 than 3.5 because landing exactly on the number is a push, not a loss. Underdog often prices its easier half-point line back out through the multiplier, which is why a half-point difference is not automatically an edge. DraftKings Pick6 is peer-to-peer, so it has no comparable probability and is never named as the place to take it; its line is shown, and marked ◆ when it differs from PrizePicks and Underdog. Those rows are listed first.
